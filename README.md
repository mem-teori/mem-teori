# M.E.M. — IDK
## Constitutional Ethics for Complex Systems

I am developing an open framework for responsible decision-making in situations where values conflict, uncertainty is real, and no option is without harm.

This work consists of two connected parts:

### M.E.M. — Mechanism • Experience • Meaning
A relational theory describing how decisions emerge from the tension between:

- Mechanisms (systems, rules, models)
- Experience (what people actually live through)
- Meaning (what is being protected or pursued)

M.E.M. is not a method, but a way of understanding complex situations where traditional optimization fails.

---

### IDK — A Constitutional Decision Framework

IDK (“I Don’t Know”) is a governance model for decisions under tragic conditions.

It does not try to find the perfect choice.
It creates structure for responsibility when no good options exist.

Key concepts include:

- Divergence — how far a decision moves from normative balance  
- Decision states — normal, review, emergency, incompatible  
- Standing debt — responsibility that persists after the decision  

The goal is not automation of ethics, but transparency and accountability.

## Why This Exists

Many modern systems make decisions that deeply affect human lives.

Yet conflicts between values are often hidden behind procedures, metrics, or code.

This project explores how systems can:

- acknowledge uncertainty
- make conflicts visible
- carry responsibility after decisions
- remain human-accountable
